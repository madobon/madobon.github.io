Based on the image provided, this is a handwritten mathematical formula representing the **gradient of a scalar field** in Cartesian coordinates.

Here is the transcription of the formula in LaTeX:

$$ \nabla \phi = \hat{i} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} + \hat{j} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} + \hat{k} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z} $$

### Explanation of the Components:

*   **$\nabla \phi$ (Del phi or Grad phi):** This represents the gradient vector of the scalar function $\phi$. The gradient points in the direction of the greatest rate of increase of the function.
*   **$\phi$ (Phi):** This is a scalar field (a function that assigns a single number to every point in space), such as temperature or electric potential.
*   **$\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y}, \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}$:** These are partial derivatives. They measure how the function $\phi$ changes as you move along the x, y, and z axes, respectively.
*   **$\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}$:** These are the standard unit vectors in the x, y, and z directions, respectively. They give the gradient its vector direction.

In summary, this equation defines how to calculate the gradient vector for a function of three variables $(x, y, z)$.
